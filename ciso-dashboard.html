Security Assessments, Compliance & vCISO Operations

Every client. Every framework. One dashboard.

CISO Dashboard is the operating platform for security assessment practices: run HIPAA, NIST, CIS, PCI, CMMC, and SAFER assessments across all your clients, turn the gaps into a budgeted roadmap, watch your clients' vendors, and hand over a board-ready report — without living in spreadsheets.

Your expertise drives every answer. AI plays a supporting role — drafting the report narrative, suggesting a roadmap order, pre-filling vendor questionnaires from their own documents — and everything it writes stays editable, versioned, and under your review.

From gap to funded project

Budget & Roadmap

Findings don't fix themselves. Price every recommendation in the Budgetary Assessment, then lay the work out on a client-wide Gantt roadmap the client can actually follow.

  • Each budget row is internal (hours × your rate) or external (dollar range), with min–max totals and a cost-split chart — exportable to Excel.
  • "Add from assessments" pulls budget rows, scoring gaps, and high/critical HIPAA risks onto the roadmap in one click.
  • Items carry workstream, priority, status, costs, and an owner picked from the client's key individuals.
  • AI "Suggest" proposes an order with a reason for each item — nothing moves until you apply it.

Your clients' vendors are your problem too

Third-Party Risk

A full vendor risk program per client, plus a roll-up across your whole practice — inventory, tiering, questionnaires, public-data signals, and findings through to closure.

  • Continuous public signals: CISA KEV, breach history, OFAC/OIG/SAM exclusions, SEC cyber incidents, sanctions lists, plus email and web security checks (SPF, DMARC, TLS, and more).
  • Vendors answer security questionnaires through an emailed link — no account — with AI pre-fill from their own SOC 2, CAIQ, or HECVAT documents, quoting page and source.
  • Findings get owners and 30/60/90-day due dates by severity, and vendors get a fix link to respond.
  • Fourth-party view shows shared providers and vendors several clients depend on.

Security & Control

A compliance platform has to clear its own bar. CISO Dashboard enforces strong sign-in, isolates every tenant, and keeps an audit trail of everything.

  • Mandatory MFA from day one: passkeys and security keys (Face ID, Touch ID, YubiKey) or authenticator app, with recovery codes and idle sign-out.
  • Strict tenant isolation, with assessors limited to their assigned clients and secrets encrypted with AES-256-GCM.
  • Uploads are malware-scanned; nightly encrypted backups go to tamper-proof storage.
  • A 12-month audit trail with CSV export — and sharing across organizations is explicit, audited, and revocable.

Your Data, Your Providers, Your Rules

CISO Dashboard keeps every client's data in one encrypted, tenant-isolated platform instead of scattered across workbooks and inboxes. Bring your own storage bucket, your own email server, and your own AI provider — Anthropic, Google, Mistral, Cloudflare, or a local model on your own hardware — each organization's keys stored encrypted and used only for its own work. And everything exports: Excel, CSV, and JSON you can re-import anywhere.

Security & Privacy By Design

Every organization on the platform is strictly isolated, with mandatory strong authentication — passkeys and hardware keys, or an authenticator app with recovery codes — and assessors scoped to only the clients they're assigned. Secrets and API keys are encrypted with AES-256-GCM with key rotation, uploads are malware-scanned, and nightly encrypted backups land in tamper-proof, object-locked storage.

Accountability is built in: a full audit trail of sign-ins, exports, sharing, and configuration changes is kept for twelve months with CSV export, and monthly archives are retained for three years. Cross-organization sharing is always explicit, notified, and revocable — nothing leaves a tenant without a deliberate decision.

The platform itself is held to the standards it measures: hardened against the OWASP baseline with enforced content-security policies, rate limiting, signed container images with software bills of materials, and recurring internal security audits.
